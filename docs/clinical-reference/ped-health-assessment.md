# PED exposure: health assessment planning reference

General clinician reference, reviewed October 22, 2026. This page contains no patient-specific note and is not a standing laboratory order. Final testing depends on the clinical history, exposure timing, examination and prior results.

## Assessment and cost priorities

- Establish the substances, doses, routes, duration, last use, concurrent supplements and ancillary drugs. Product names/photos may clarify uncertain exposure.
- Review symptoms, blood pressure, cardiovascular risk, mental health, fertility goals and injection practices as relevant.
- Review existing results to avoid duplication. Patient-paid labs require a patient-specific insurance/self-pay estimate before ordering; do not assume membership includes testing.
- Normal laboratory results do not guarantee absence of injury, particularly cardiovascular disease. Decide on ECG or further cardiac assessment from exposure, symptoms and findings.

## Initial laboratory considerations

| Test | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| CBC | Hemoglobin/hematocrit elevation associated with androgen use; anemia and other blood-count abnormalities. |
| CMP | Creatinine/eGFR, electrolytes, AST/ALT, alkaline phosphatase, bilirubin and albumin for initial kidney/liver evaluation. |
| Lipid panel | Cardiovascular risk and androgen-associated HDL/LDL changes. |
| Urinalysis | Blood/protein that may warrant kidney evaluation. |

## Selective additions

- TSH with reflex free T4 for thyroid symptoms or possible thyroid-drug exposure. Testosterone exposure alone does not automatically require a thyroid panel.
- Cystatin C when muscle mass or creatine use complicates interpretation of creatinine-based kidney estimates.
- Urine albumin/creatinine ratio if proteinuria or other kidney-risk findings warrant it.
- HbA1c when diabetes risk or possible growth-hormone exposure warrants it.
- Testosterone and LH/FSH for endocrine assessment; timing depends on current/recent agents and last dose. Do not diagnose primary testosterone deficiency from an exposure-confounded result alone.

Heavy training, creatine use and hydration can affect interpretation. Additional testing, referrals and repeat intervals must be selected by the treating clinician. This list does not replace history, examination or assessment of urgent symptoms.

## Record handling

Keep identifiable encounter notes in Charm or a private clinician-controlled working file outside this public repository. Drafts must distinguish clinician-reported facts from proposed testing, missing information and established findings. Do not treat undocumented symptoms as denied. A planning reference is not evidence that an order was placed or transmitted.
